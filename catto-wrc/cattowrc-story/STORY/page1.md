# Page 1: The Weight of Arithmetic

The championship had been won through mathematics.

Kuro understood this. Two titles built on consistency—never fastest on any single stage, but always fast enough multiplied by eighteen stages, thirteen rallies, two seasons. The algorithm was simple: minimize risk, maximize points accumulation, let rivals destroy themselves chasing tenths of seconds that evaporated in retirement smoke.

Toyota understood this too. Gave him the number one car. Extended his contract through 2028. Built their team philosophy around his methodical brilliance—kaizen applied to rally sport, continuous improvement measured in championship trophies rather than stage wins.

The mathematics were elegant.

The mathematics were suffocating.

Kuro sat in the Warsaw hotel room at 02:17, Poland won six hours ago, flight to Australia departing in four hours. Laptop displayed SS14 Mikołajki telemetry with forensic clarity. His racing line traced in red. Evans' optimal line in blue. The gap between them measured in tenths of seconds and choices made.

The fast left-hand crest at kilometer seven-point-three.

Ninety percent throttle. Safe compression. Second fastest time.

Evans had stayed flat. Gained three-point-six seconds. Won the stage. Collected no championship points because Evans drove for different mathematics—rally victories instead of title arithmetic.

Kuro's tail wrapped tighter around his waist.

"Still awake." Akira's voice from the doorway. His co-driver held two cups of coffee, steam curling in the dim light. "We have established this is unhealthy pattern."

"Reviewing data."

"You have reviewed SS14 eleven times since we returned from service park." Akira set one cup beside the laptop. Black coffee, no sugar. Exactly as Kuro preferred. "The telemetry has not changed in six hours. Your line was correct for championship protection. Evans' line was faster for stage victory. Both things are true simultaneously."

Kuro's copper eyes stayed fixed on the screen. The crest approach. The moment of decision. Ninety percent throttle versus full commitment.

"Toyota expects championship," he said.

"Toyota expects you to drive." Akira settled into the chair across from him. "There is difference between these expectations."

"The outcome is identical."

"The feeling is not."

Kuro's finger traced the racing line on the screen. Red path diverging from blue. Safe versus optimal. Protected versus committed.

Twenty-five point lead over Thierry. Sami forty-one back. Two rallies remaining.

He closed the laptop.

"Pack for Australia," he said. "Wheels up at oh-six-thirty."

"You should sleep."

"Cannot."

Akira studied him. Twenty-three rallies together. Forty-seven stage wins. Two championships. The co-driver knew Kuro's rhythms better than the pace notes—knew when silence meant calculation and when silence meant something darker.

"The mathematics are comfortable," Akira said quietly.

Kuro's hand moved to his chest. Found the omamori beneath his shirt. Small silk pouch from Ise Grand Shrine, blessed by priests who understood that some protections couldn't be measured in stage times and championship points.

Prayer for victory.

Prayer for safety.

Prayer for something he couldn't name but felt pressing against his ribs every time he lifted off throttle before a crest and watched rivals gain time through commitment he'd calculated away.

"Latvala called," Akira continued. "Wants private meeting when we arrive Sydney. Before recce. Before media."

"About what?"

"He did not specify." Akira's expression stayed neutral. "But team principals do not request private meetings at four AM arrivals when everything is satisfactory."

Through the hotel window, Warsaw lights spread across Polish night. City that had watched him drive to second place. Sufficient result. Protected mathematics. Victory that felt like something other than triumph.

Kuro stood. Moved to the window. Pressed his palm against glass cold enough to sting.

"Monte-Carlo SS1," he said. "January. Do you remember?"

"I remember every stage."

"I drove it committed. Full throttle through the uncertain sections. Trusted the notes. Trusted the car. Trusted—" He paused. Three seconds. Exact calculation even now. "Trusted myself."

"You finished second on the stage. Three-point-two seconds behind Sami."

"But it felt like victory."

Akira said nothing. Let the statement settle.

Kuro watched Warsaw. Thought about Monte-Carlo in January when the championship was fresh possibility instead of mathematical protection. When stages were dances instead of arithmetic. When the omamori felt like blessing instead of permission to be afraid.

"Poland SS14," he said. "Did not feel like victory."

"No."

"The mathematics say otherwise."

"Mathematics measure outcomes. Not meaning." Akira stood. Joined him at the window. "Latvala knows this. He drove thirteen years. Seventeen wins. One championship. Retired at thirty-four because the hunger vanished and mathematics replaced mythology."

"You think he sees the same in me."

"I think he sees driver who won two championships through brilliance and now protects third championship through arithmetic that feels safer than trust." Akira's reflection met Kuro's in the glass. "I think he wants to know if The Shadow still remembers how to hunt instead of hide."

The words landed precisely. Surgical strike against defenses built from consistent results and protected leads.

Kuro's tail unwrapped slightly. Copper eyes caught Warsaw lights, reflecting patterns that looked almost like uncertainty.

"Australia is fast gravel," he said. "Flowing stages. Natural rhythm terrain."

"Yes."

"Evans will be fast. Home rally advantage."

"Yes."

"Thierry will attack. Twenty-five points requires miracles."

"Yes."

"And I will do what?" The question emerged quieter than intended. "Drive ninety-five percent? Collect sufficient points? Win championship through survival mathematics?"

Akira didn't answer immediately. Let the question breathe.

"You will make choice," he finally said. "Same choice you have made every stage since championship fight began. Protection or performance. Arithmetic or instinct. Safety or—"

"Risk."

"Faith."

Kuro looked at his co-driver. Twenty-three rallies. Two championships. Partnership built on precision and trust that had calcified somewhere into precision and calculation.

"If I attack and lose time—"

"Then we discuss in service and adjust. This is rally sport. This is what we do." Akira's expression softened. "But I think we both know you will not lose time if you drive instead of calculate. Talent that won two championships does not disappear when you choose performance over protection. It just gets buried under mathematics that feel safer than truth."

"What truth?"

"That you are afraid of what happens when you drive at limit and discover whether talent matches the reputation you have built by never fully testing it."

The statement hung between them. Not accusation. Observation delivered with co-driver precision—accurate pace note for navigator reading terrain he couldn't see himself.

Kuro's hand pressed harder against the window. Glass holding back Warsaw cold. Glass protecting him from consequences. Glass that felt increasingly like cage.

His phone vibrated. Screen lit with message preview:

**Yuki:** *Congratulations on Poland! When will you call? Miss you, brother.*

He let the screen go dark. Couldn't parse sister's observations tonight. Couldn't field questions about championships and choices and the mathematics that explained everything except why victory felt hollow.

"Four hours until departure," Akira said. "You should attempt rest."

"Will not sleep."

"Will not stop calculating."

Kuro's reflection stared back. The Shadow. Two-time champion. Winner through arithmetic that had conquered rally sport and now felt like the thing preventing him from understanding what rally sport meant beyond points accumulation.

"Latvala wants to talk before recce," he said.

"Yes."

"He will ask about SS14. About the lift before the crest. About the three-point-six seconds to Evans."

"Probably."

"He will ask if I am driving to win championships or driving to not lose them."

Akira nodded slowly. "And what will you answer?"

Kuro watched Warsaw. City that didn't care about rally championships or the weight of arithmetic or the question that followed him from Monte-Carlo through Sweden through Portugal through Poland—gaining points, collecting trophies, winning through mathematics that felt less like victory each rally despite evidence declaring otherwise.

"I do not know," he said.

The admission cost something. Precision driver acknowledging uncertainty. Calculator admitting variables he couldn't solve.

Twenty-five point lead over Thierry. Sami forty-one back. Two rallies remaining.

The numbers were comfortable.

That was the problem.

Warsaw lights blurred slightly. Kuro blinked. Realized he'd been staring without focus for seventeen seconds. Uncommon lapse. Unacceptable imprecision.

"Pack for Australia," he said again. "Long flight. Unfamiliar continent. Final test before Japan."

Akira moved toward his room. Paused at the door.

"The omamori," he said. "What did you pray for at Ise?"

Kuro's hand found the silk pouch. Felt priests' blessing woven into fabric. Protection granted. Victory delivered. Championship approaching through arithmetic that should have felt like answered prayer.

"Victory," he said.

"And?"

"Safety."

"And?"

The question lingered. Akira waited. Warsaw stayed indifferent beyond the glass.

"I do not remember," Kuro finally admitted. "Third prayer. Cannot recall the words."

Akira's expression shifted. Understanding something Kuro couldn't see himself.

"Perhaps Australia is where you remember," he said. Closed the door. Left Kuro alone with Warsaw and laptop and telemetry that showed the truth he couldn't escape:

SS14 Mikołajki. The fast left crest. Ninety percent throttle.

Safe.

Sufficient.

Second fastest.

Not victory.

Kuro opened the laptop again. Stared at the racing lines. Red path of protection. Blue path of commitment. The mathematics that separated them clear and absolute and meaningless for explaining the weight pressing against his chest.

Outside, Warsaw prepared for dawn. Inside, champion prepared for flight to unfamiliar continent where team principal waited with questions and recce notes waited with answers and SS1 Australia waited with choice he'd been avoiding since the championship became something to protect instead of something to chase.

His tail wrapped tight around his waist.

Copper eyes dimmed.

The Shadow sat in Warsaw darkness, studying telemetry from Poland victory, calculating nothing, feeling everything, waiting for departure to Australia where the mathematics were comfortable and Latvala would ask the question Kuro already knew he couldn't answer:

Do you deserve it if you never risk losing it?

Four hours until flight.

Twenty-five point lead.

Two rallies remaining.

The arithmetic was elegant.

The arithmetic was prison.

Kuro closed the laptop. Started packing for Australia. Precise folding, optimized weight distribution, everything calculated and protected and insufficient for the question that followed him from Warsaw to Sydney:

What happens when you forget the third prayer?

What happens when protection becomes the cage?

What happens when The Shadow learns to hunt again?

Australia waited with the answer.

Kuro packed in darkness, avoiding mirrors, postponing dawn, protecting mathematics that couldn't protect him from the truth approaching with geometric precision:

Something had to change.

Or the third championship would taste exactly like Poland SS14.

Sufficient.

Safe.

Hollow.

Not victory.

Not anymore.
